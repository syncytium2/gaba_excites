Riz M, Braun M, Pedersen MG (2014) Mathematical modeling of heterogeneous electrophysiological responses in
human β-cells. *PLoS Comput Biol* 10:e1003389. doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003389 · PMID 24391482 · PMC3879095.

Pedersen MG (2010) A biophysical model of electrical activity in human β-cells. *Biophys J* 99:3200–3207.
doi:10.1016/j.bpj.2010.09.004 · PMID 21081067 · PMC2980734.

This is everything `src/core/human.ts` and `rizPool` in `src/core/pools.ts` take from the two papers, and how it
was read.

## How the papers were read (2026-11-02)

- **Text, equations and Table 1** from the PDFs the owner downloaded (now in the library), by `pdftotext -layout`.
  That tool drops the minus sign of this font in some places ("VmCaL 25" for −25 mV). Every sign below was read
  against the equations' text, the figure axes, and the authors' code; none was taken from the text layer alone.
- **The authors' XPP code** (`PLoSCompBiol_Fig1.ode`, Riz 2014 Software S1, and `human-BJ2010.ode`, Pedersen
  2010, from www.dei.unipd.it/~pedersen) was read to settle signs, for the initial values, and to check the port.
  None of it is in this repository. Permission to use the model was asked of Pedersen on 2026-11-02; the build
  went ahead on the owner's instruction to assume a yes (docs/process.md).
- **Pedersen 2010's Supporting Material** (Document S1, Figs. S1–S3; PMC2980734 `mmc1.pdf`, no DOI of its own) was
  read 2026-11-02, after the first build. It is where the reversal potentials of the voltage-clamp fits are stated
  (below). Braun et al. 2008's own figures have not been read for numbers yet.

## The reversal potentials: fitting versus firing (Pedersen 2010 Supporting Material)

The currents were fitted to Braun et al. 2008's voltage-clamp I–V curves with reversal potentials different from those
every simulation of electrical activity uses (Table 1). The supplement's own words:

- Fig. S1B: "Assuming a reversal potential of VCa = 40 mV (3) allowed decent fitting to the Ca²⁺-currents measured by
  Braun et al. (3)." Fig. S1C: "All simulations of electrical activity were done with VCa = 65 mV".
- Fig. S2B: "Assuming a reversal potential of VNa = 100 mV allowed decent fitting to the Na⁺-currents measured by
  Braun et al. (3) ... However, all simulations of electrical activity were done with VNa = 70 mV, which yields smaller
  Na⁺-currents".
- Fig. S3B: the Kv current fitted to Braun's cobalt-resistant K⁺ current with V_K = −75 mV (the text layer drops the
  sign; a K⁺ reversal of +75 is impossible and the figure's current is outward at positive V); the BK current, with
  V_Ca = 40 mV in its −I_Ca term, reproduces the iberiotoxin-sensitive current. Fig. S3C: with V_Ca = 65 mV the BK
  and total K⁺ currents are larger at positive voltages.

Separately, the main text's Fig. 1A uses V_Ca = 50 mV to fit the inactivation of total Ca²⁺ current measured by Kelly
et al. 1991, the source of the L-type inactivation's Φ = 57 mV.

So a voltage-clamp lesson meant to reproduce Braun's I–V curves would run this cell with V_Ca = +40 mV and V_Na =
+100 mV; the app's model uses the firing values (+65, +70), as both papers' simulations do.

## Equations (Riz 2014 numbering; Pedersen 2010's in brackets)

Currents in pA/pF, conductances in nS/pF, V in mV, t in ms, Ca in µM.

```
(13)  dV/dt = −(I_SK + I_BK + I_Kv + I_HERG + I_Na + I_CaL + I_CaPQ + I_CaT + I_K(ATP) + I_leak + I_GABAR)
(16)  I_SK    = g_SK · Ca_m^n/(K_SK^n + Ca_m^n) · (V − V_K)
(17)  I_BK    = g_BK · m_BK · (−I_Ca(V) + B_BK) · (V − V_K)                 [Eq. 9; g_BK in nS/pA]
(18)  I_Kv    = g_Kv · m_Kv · (V − V_K)
(19)  I_HERG  = g_HERG · m_HERG · h_HERG · (V − V_K)
(20)  I_Na    = g_Na · m_Na,∞(V) · h_Na · (V − V_Na)
(21)  I_CaL   = g_CaL · m_CaL,∞(V) · h_CaL · (V − V_Ca)
(22)  I_CaPQ  = g_CaPQ · m_CaPQ,∞(V) · (V − V_Ca)
(23)  I_CaT   = g_CaT · m_CaT,∞(V) · h_CaT · (V − V_Ca)
(24)  I_K(ATP) = g_K(ATP) · (V − V_K)
(25)  I_leak  = g_leak · (V − V_leak)
(26)  I_GABAR = g_GABAR · (V − V_Cl)
(27)  I_Ca    = I_CaL + I_CaPQ + I_CaT
(28)  dx/dt   = (x_∞(V) − x)/τ_x
(29)  x_∞(V)  = 1/(1 + exp((V − V_x)/n_x))          activation n < 0, inactivation n > 0
(30)  h_CaL,∞ = max(0, min(1, 1 + m_CaL,∞(V)·(V − V_Ca)/57 mV))   [Eq. 7, Φ = 57 mV "adjusted"]
(31)  τ_mKv   = τ_mKv,0 + 10·exp((−20 mV − V)/6 mV) ms for V ≥ −26.6 mV;  τ_mKv,0 + 30 ms below   [Eq. 8]
(14)  dCa_m/dt = f·α·Cm·(−I_CaL − I_CaPQ − I_CaT)/Vol_m − f·(Vol_c/Vol_m)·[B·(Ca_m − Ca_c) + J_PMCA + J_NCX]
(15)  dCa_c/dt = f·[B·(Ca_m − Ca_c) − J_SERCA + J_leak]
(32)  J_SERCA = J_SERCA,max · Ca_c²/(K_SERCA² + Ca_c²)
(33)  J_PMCA  = J_PMCA,max · Ca_m/(K_PMCA + Ca_m)
(34)  J_NCX   = J_NCX,0 · Ca_m
```

Activations of Na⁺ and all three Ca²⁺ currents are instantaneous (m = m_∞(V)), because "the total Ca²⁺ current
activated very rapidly (<1 ms)" and Na⁺ kinetics are rapid (Pedersen 2010). SK is instantaneous in Ca_m. BK's
Ca²⁺ dependence is instantaneous in I_Ca; its voltage gate follows Eq. 28.

## Where the port departs from the papers

- **Instantaneous activation** (Na⁺, the three Ca²⁺ currents) is a gate with τ = 1e-9 ms, set to m_∞ at the new V
  each step, as for the mouse beta cell: the conductance lags V by one step (0.01 ms).
- **BK reads I_Ca at the start of the step**, from the Ca²⁺ channels' gates and V there, through the solver's
  `iCa` input (`Channel.readsICa`), and is written as conductance g_BK·B_BK times m_BK·(−I_Ca + B_BK)/B_BK.
- **τ_mKv** follows the paper's piecewise Eq. 31; the code's `min(ln 3, …)` differs by the 0.13% jump at −26.6 mV.
- **Units:** densities × 10 pF give nS and pA; the app's Cm control scales every conductance with Cm.
- **Not ported:** the glycolytic component (Eqs. 35–47, Fig. 6); the conductance noise of Fig. 1E.
